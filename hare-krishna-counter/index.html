The Hare Krishna maha-mantra

The maha-mantra ("great mantra") is: Hare Krishna Hare Krishna Krishna Krishna Hare Hare, Hare Rama Hare Rama Rama Rama Hare Hare. It consists of sixteen names and thirty-two syllables and appears in the Kali-Santarana Upanishad, which presents it as the essential practice for the present age. It was widely popularised by Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu in the sixteenth century and later spread across the world by the Gaudiya Vaishnava movement.

Understanding the words

  • Hare – a call to Hara, the divine energy of the Lord, often understood as Radha.
  • Krishna – "all-attractive", the Supreme Person.
  • Rama – "the source of all pleasure", used here to address the Lord.

Devotees explain that chanting is like a child calling for its mother: the mantra is a sincere call rather than a request for something specific.

How rounds work: 16 rounds of japa

In ISKCON and many Gaudiya Vaishnava communities, a "round" means one pass through all 108 beads of the japa mala, chanting the full maha-mantra on each bead. Initiated devotees commit to a daily number of rounds; the common standard is sixteen rounds, which takes roughly two hours at a steady pace. Beginners often start with one or two rounds and slowly increase.

Chanting and kirtan

The maha-mantra is sung in congregational kirtan with mridanga and kartals, and chanted quietly in japa. Both are recommended in the tradition. Use the counter for the quiet japa, and enjoy kirtan without counting. Japa is often described as a personal conversation with Krishna, and kirtan as a loud public celebration.

Source, pronunciation and method of the maha-mantra

Source: The sixteen-word Hare Krishna maha-mantra is named in the Kali-Santarana Upanishad (attached to the Krishna Yajurveda), which presents it as the remedy for the troubles of the age of Kali. In the 16th century Chaitanya Mahaprabhu made congregational chanting (sankirtan) of these names a mass movement in Bengal and Odisha, and the Gaudiya tradition continues it today.

Pronunciation: Hare is said "HAH-ray", Krishna "KRISH-na", Rama "RAH-ma". The mantra is spoken as 16 words and 32 syllables in the order Hare Krishna ×2, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare, then Hare Rama ×2, Rama Rama, Hare Hare.

Method: Gaudiya practice is 16 rounds daily on a 108-bead tulsi mala, which is 1,728 mantras (counted by the full mantra, not by word). Count one tap per complete mantra here, and expect each round to take roughly 6–8 minutes at a calm speed.

What practitioners say it gives: a sense of devotion and steadiness through the day. These are devotional, not medical or guaranteed, results.

Frequently asked questions

How many rounds of Hare Krishna should I chant daily?

Initiated devotees commonly chant sixteen rounds of 108. Beginners can start with one round and gradually increase. Follow the guidance of your spiritual teacher if you have one.

Does one bead mean one Hare Krishna or the full mantra?

In the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition, each bead is one full chanting of the sixteen-name maha-mantra. Tap this counter once after each full mantra.

Is the Hare Krishna counter free?

Yes. It is free, requires no sign-up and stores data only in your browser.

Can I use the counter for kirtan?

You may count for personal japa. Kirtan is usually enjoyed without counting, but you can count if you are fulfilling a vow.

What time is best for chanting Hare Krishna?

Early morning, especially Brahma Muhurta, is traditionally recommended, though the mantra can be chanted at any time.
