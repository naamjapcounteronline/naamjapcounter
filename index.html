Japa Counter FAQ

What is a naam japa counter?

A naam japa counter is a digital tool that counts every repetition of a name or mantra such as Radha, Ram or Om Namah Shivaya, so you can focus on chanting instead of counting. This counter tracks each round of 108 automatically.

Is this japa counter online free?

Yes. The counter, digital mala, calculators and trackers are completely free, need no login, and your progress is saved privately in your own browser.

Why do we count japa in 108?

A traditional japa mala has 108 beads plus one guru bead (sumeru). 108 is considered a sacred number in Vedic and yogic traditions, so one full round of the mala equals 108 chants.

Can I use this jaap counter on my phone?

Yes. It works in any modern mobile browser and can be installed to your home screen like an app. Use the large tap button, vibration feedback and keep-screen-on option while you chant.

How do I do naam jap correctly?

Sit comfortably, calm your breath, choose one name or mantra, chant it with attention at a steady pace, count with a mala or counter, and end with a moment of silence and gratitude. See our step-by-step guide on how to do naam jap.

What is the best time for japa?

Brahma Muhurta, about 96 to 48 minutes before sunrise, is traditionally best. Use our Brahma Muhurta time tomorrow calculator for your city. Any quiet time you can practise regularly is also good.

What is a mantra counter and how is it different from a mala counter?

A mantra counter counts repetitions of any mantra, while a mala counter specifically shows the 108 beads of a mala. This site combines both in one digital mala.

Can I count my own mantra or name?

Yes. Choose the Custom chip, type any name or mantra, and the counter works exactly the same way.

Does the chanting counter work offline?

After the page has loaded once, counting keeps working without internet because everything runs in your browser.

Is my japa data stored on a server?

No. Counts, streaks and settings are stored only in your browser's local storage on your own device.
