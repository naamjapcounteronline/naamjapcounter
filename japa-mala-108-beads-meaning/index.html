Japa Mala 108 Beads – Meaning, Types And How To Use

Why 108? This guide explains the traditional meanings of the 108 beads of a japa mala, the sumeru bead, mala materials and correct handling.

Why does a mala have 108 beads?

The number 108 appears across Hindu, Buddhist and Jain traditions. There is no single universally accepted reason; several explanations are given, some symbolic and some astronomical.

  • Sacred completeness. 1 stands for the One (the Absolute), 0 for emptiness, 8 for infinity; together a symbol of the whole.
  • Sanskrit alphabet. Sanskrit has 54 letters, each with masculine (Shiva) and feminine (Shakti) forms: 54 × 2 = 108.
  • Sri Yantra. Described as having 54 intersections of lines, each with two aspects: 108 points.
  • The Sun and Earth. The Sun is about 108 times its own diameter from Earth, and its diameter is around 108 times Earth's. Devotees see this as cosmic harmony.
  • 108 names. Many deities have an ashtottara shatanamavali, a list of 108 names.
  • Upanishads. The muktika canon lists 108 Upanishads.
  • Practicality. A round of 108 takes roughly five to eight minutes, a manageable unit.

The sumeru (guru) bead

The additional large bead, often with a tassel, is the sumeru, meaning "mountain" (Mount Meru). It marks the beginning and end of each round. It is never crossed; when you reach it, you turn the mala around and continue in the opposite direction.

How to hold and use a mala

  1. Hold with the right hand, draped over the middle finger.
  2. Use the thumb to roll each bead towards you.
  3. Avoid using the index finger.
  4. Chant once per bead.
  5. At the sumeru, turn the mala and continue.
  6. Keep the mala clean and respectfully stored.

Digital mala

When you cannot carry a mala, use the digital mala. It lights 108 beads and rings a bell at the end of a round.

Frequently asked questions

Why is 108 considered sacred?

It appears in many traditions as a number of completeness, linked to names of deities, Sanskrit letters and astronomical ratios.

What is the sumeru bead?

The larger guide bead that marks the start and end of a round. It is not crossed.

Can I use a mala of 27 or 54 beads?

Yes. Some malas have 27 or 54 beads, used for several rounds to make 108.

Which mala is best?

Choose according to your tradition or what you feel drawn to; rudraksha for Shiva, tulsi for Vishnu forms, etc.

Is a digital mala acceptable?

It is a practical aid. Many people use both, according to convenience.
