Why a 1 to 108 chart?

A numbered chart is a simple visual aid for people who count japa by hand, practise kara mala or teach children to count to 108. It also makes a pleasant printed card to keep near your altar or mala.

108 in Hindi and Sanskrit

108 is written १०८ in Devanagari numerals. In Hindi it is "ek sau aath" (एक सौ आठ) and in Sanskrit "ashtottara-shatam" (अष्टोत्तरशतम्), meaning "one hundred and eight". You will see "Ashtottara Shatanamavali" used for a list of 108 names of a deity.

Patterns inside 108

  • 108 = 4 × 27 = 2 × 54 = 12 × 9 (twelve zodiac houses × nine planets/grahas in one traditional explanation).
  • Digits add up to 9 (1 + 0 + 8), a number regarded as complete.
  • The checkpoints at 27, 54 and 81 split the mala into quarters.

Frequently asked questions

How do I count 1 to 108 in Hindi?

Count एक, दो, तीन and so on up to एक सौ आठ. The chart above shows Devanagari numerals; hover a number to see its Hindi digits.

Why is 108 significant?

It is the number of beads on a mala and is considered complete and sacred in several traditions. See our guide on 108 beads.
