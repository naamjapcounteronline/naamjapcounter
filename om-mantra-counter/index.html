What is Om chanting?

Om (ॐ, also written Aum) is described in the Mandukya Upanishad as the sound of all existence. Many traditions begin and end prayers, yoga and meditation with it. A single Om may last the whole length of an exhalation, so people often count fewer Oms than other mantras.

Step-by-step Om practice

  1. Sit with a straight back and relax the jaw.
  2. Inhale gently through the nose.
  3. On the exhale chant "Aaa–Ooo–Mmm" in one steady sound.
  4. Tap the counter once, rest for one breath, repeat.
  5. End with a minute of silence.

Frequently asked questions

How many times should I chant Om?

Beginners can start with 3 to 11 Oms and slowly build to 21 or 108. Quality of attention matters more than the number.

Is Om a mantra or a sound?

Om is both a sacred syllable and the root of many mantras. It can be chanted alone or at the start of a mantra.

Can I use the Om drone while counting?

Yes. Turn on the Om drone in the audio panel; it plays while you tap or use auto mode.

What if I run out of breath?

Chant softer and shorter, and breathe in fully between Oms. There is no need to strain.

Is the count saved?

Yes, in your browser on this device only.
