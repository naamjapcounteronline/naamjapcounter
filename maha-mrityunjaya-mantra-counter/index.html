About the Maha Mrityunjaya mantra

The Maha Mrityunjaya mantra, also called the Tryambakam mantra, is addressed to Lord Shiva as the three-eyed one. It appears in the Rig Veda (7.59.12) and the Yajur Veda. The name means "the great mantra that conquers death", but teachers explain this more as liberation from fear and bondage than as avoiding physical death.

Word-by-word meaning

WordMeaning
TryambakamThe three-eyed one (Shiva)
YajamaheWe worship
SugandhimFragrant
PushtivardhanamNourisher, increaser of wellbeing
Urvarukam ivaLike a ripe cucumber
BandhanatFrom the stem / bondage
Mrityor mukshiyaMay we be freed from death
Ma amritatNot from immortality

The image is of a ripe cucumber that effortlessly detaches from its vine. The prayer asks to be freed from fear and attachment in the same effortless way, and to be established in the immortal.

How to chant using the counter

  1. Sit calmly in a quiet place. A picture of Shiva, a lamp or a Shivalinga can help focus.
  2. Decide your count: 108 is common; some take a vow of 1008 or 1,25,000 in total.
  3. Chant clearly at a measured pace; one full mantra is one tap.
  4. If you are chanting for someone's wellbeing, hold them in your heart as you chant.
  5. Close with a moment of silence.

Long vows can be planned using the mantra calculator.

A note on health and expectations

Many people chant this mantra during illness or hard times for peace and courage. Please remember it is a spiritual practice that can be a comfort alongside, not instead of, proper medical care. We do not claim that it cures disease.

Best days

Mondays, Pradosh, Maha Shivaratri and the month of Shravan are particularly dear to Shiva devotees. You can chant any time you need strength. Early morning practice can be timed using the Brahma Muhurta calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How many times should I chant the Maha Mrityunjaya mantra?

Commonly 108 times daily; some devotees do 1008 or take a vow of 1.25 lakh. Choose a number you can do with attention.

Is this mantra for health?

Many people chant it for peace and the wellbeing of loved ones. It is a spiritual practice and not a substitute for medical treatment.

What does Maha Mrityunjaya mean?

It means the great (maha) mantra that conquers (jaya) death (mrityu), understood as liberation from fear and bondage.

Is this Maha Mrityunjaya counter free?

Yes. It is free, with no sign-up, and your data stays in your browser.

Which day is best for this mantra?

Monday and Pradosh are traditional, but it can be chanted any day.
