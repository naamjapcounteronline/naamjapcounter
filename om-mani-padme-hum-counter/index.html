About Om Mani Padme Hum

This six-syllable mantra is associated with Avalokiteshvara (Chenrezig), the Buddha of compassion, and is widely recited in Tibetan Buddhism. It is carved on prayer stones and written on prayer wheels. Practitioners often recite it in sets of 108, 1,000 or more, sometimes as an accumulation of millions.

Traditional commentaries link the six syllables to six perfections (generosity, ethics, patience, effort, concentration, wisdom). Explanations differ between teachers; follow your own lineage's instructions.

How to recite

  1. Sit calmly and generate a kind intention.
  2. Recite the mantra softly, one full recitation per tap.
  3. When the bell rings, pause and dedicate the merit to all beings.

Frequently asked questions

What does Om Mani Padme Hum mean?

It is commonly translated as "the jewel in the lotus" and is associated with compassion; teachers give deeper explanations.

How many times should I recite it?

Many practitioners recite 108 times daily. Larger accumulations are taken up in retreats.

Is it counted by syllable or by recitation?

Usually by full recitation. Tap once after each Om Mani Padme Hum.

Can I add the Tibetan or Sanskrit text?

Yes, custom text is supported, though the preset already includes it.
