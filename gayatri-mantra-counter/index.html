About the Gayatri mantra

The Gayatri mantra is one of the oldest and most revered Vedic mantras. It is found in the Rig Veda (3.62.10) and is attributed to the sage Vishvamitra. It is composed in the Gayatri metre of 24 syllables and addressed to Savitr, the divine power of the Sun, as the inspirer of the intellect. Many people chant it as part of daily sandhya vandana.

Word-by-word meaning

WordMeaning
OmThe primordial sound
Bhur Bhuvah SvahEarth, atmosphere (mind), heaven (spirit) – the three realms
TatThat (the Supreme)
SavituhOf Savitr, the divine source/sun
VarenyamMost excellent, worthy of choice
Bhargo DevasyaThe radiance of the divine
DhimahiWe meditate upon
Dhiyo Yo Nah PrachodayatMay it inspire our intellects

A popular rendering is: "We meditate on the radiant glory of that divine source; may it illuminate our minds."

How to use this Gayatri mantra counter

  1. Chant after bathing if possible, facing east at sunrise.
  2. Take a few deep breaths. Try the sama vritti pranayama exercise.
  3. Chant the mantra at a steady pace; one full chant equals one tap.
  4. 108 repetitions is common, and many chant 3, 11 or 27 times for shorter practice.
  5. Close in silence for a few breaths.

Gayatri japa has specific traditional guidelines on initiation and pronunciation. Please follow your family or guru's method if you have one.

Sandhya times

The mantra is traditionally chanted at the three sandhyas: dawn, noon and dusk. Dawn practice before sunrise is especially valued. Find your local sunrise and the preceding window with the Brahma Muhurta time tomorrow tool.

Learning pronunciation

Pronunciation matters in Vedic chanting. Listen to a trusted teacher or recording, then practise slowly. Start with a few repetitions each day rather than a long session.

The Gayatri mantra: source, words and pronunciation

Source: The Gayatri Mantra is Rigveda 3.62.10, attributed to the sage Vishvamitra and addressed to Savitr, the sun as the inspirer of understanding. It is in the Gayatri metre of three lines of eight syllables, 24 in all.

Words: Om bhur bhuvah svah, tat savitur varenyam, bhargo devasya dhimahi, dhiyo yo nah prachodayat. A common reading is: we meditate on the radiance of the divine sun; may it inspire our intellect.

Pronunciation: bhur-BHU-vah SVAH; tat sa-vi-TUR va-REN-yam; BHAR-go de-VAS-ya dhee-MAH-hee; dhee-YO yo NAH pra-cho-DA-yat. Practice slowly with a teacher if you can.

Method: Traditionally recited at the three sandhyas (dawn, noon and dusk), often in sets of 3, 9, 27 or 108. One full recitation is one count, so this counter helps most with the 108 set.

Frequently asked questions

How many times should I chant the Gayatri mantra?

Common practices are 3, 11, 27 or 108 times. Choose a number you can sustain with attention.

Who composed the Gayatri mantra?

Tradition attributes it to the sage Vishvamitra; it appears in the Rig Veda (3.62.10).

What is the best time to chant the Gayatri mantra?

Sunrise, noon and sunset are the traditional sandhya times, with sunrise considered especially auspicious.

Is this Gayatri mantra counter free?

Yes. It is free, with no login, and your counts are stored only in your browser.

Can anyone chant the Gayatri mantra?

Practices differ among traditions. Many modern teachers welcome everyone to chant it with respect. Follow your own tradition's guidance if you have one.
