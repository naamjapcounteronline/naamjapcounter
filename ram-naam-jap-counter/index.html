The meditative science of Ram naam japa

In devotional literature, the two syllables Ra and Ma hold a distinct meditative stature. Rather than requiring complex theological study or ceremonial paraphernalia, repeating the sacred name of Ram serves as an accessible anchor for the restless mind. Classical practitioners note that vocalizing "Ra" opens the respiratory passage, while "Ma" gently seals the lips, making it an ideal companion for conscious breath awareness throughout normal daily tasks.

Whether chanted audibly (Vaikhari) to dissolve intrusive thoughts or contemplated in mental silence (Manasika) during meditation, Ram naam practice cultivates inner fortitude, moral clarity, and unbroken tranquility.

Popular variations of Ram mantra for personal chanting

  • Ram Ram: The essential Dvadasa-syllable root, favored for continuous rhythm during walking, commuting, or silent breath-synchronized contemplation.
  • Shri Ram Jai Ram Jai Jai Ram: The revered thirteen-syllable Taraka mantra widely practiced in Maharashtra and North India, known for its melodic cadence and heart-centering rhythm.
  • Om Shri Ramaya Namah: A formal Vedic salutation commonly used during dedicated seated japa, morning altar worship, and offering rituals.
  • Sita Ram / Siyaram: Invoking the harmonious balance of divine grace and righteous action, honoring the divine couple in devotional communion.

Ram naam likhit japa (the practice of sacred handwriting)

Beyond oral recitation, the tradition of likhit japa—handwriting the sacred name "राम" repeatedly in dedicated notebooks—is revered across pilgrimage centers. The kinetic discipline of physically scripting each character calms ocular distraction and aligns physical movement with mental remembrance. Ram naam in scripture, and how to say it

Source: Tulsidas devotes a long passage of the Ramcharitmanas (Bala Kanda) to praising the name "Ram" as greater than the form. The same idea is expressed in the verse often recited at the end of the Vishnu Sahasranama, "Sri Rama Rama Rameti…", which says that one utterance of Ram equals a thousand names.

Pronunciation: Ram is said "RAAM" with a long first vowel; the full chant is Shri Ram Jai Ram Jai Jai Ram. The Taraka mantra is "Shri Ram Jai Ram Jai Jai Ram", usually repeated in the same rhythm.

Method: Ram naam is used for japa on a tulsi mala, for writing (Ram naam lekhan) and by families chanting together. Many people keep a daily count of 108 or 1,008, and use Tuesday, Saturday and Ram Navami for extra rounds.

Tradition notes: Mahatma Gandhi is remembered for taking "Ram" as his steadying name; whether you follow that story or not, a short name is easy to keep with each breath.

Frequently asked questions

How many times should I do Ram naam jap?

Many people chant 108 times daily. Some take a vow of 11, 21 or 108 malas, or one lakh names. Choose what you can sustain with sincerity.

Which is better, Ram Ram or Shri Ram Jai Ram Jai Jai Ram?

Both are respected. "Ram Ram" is simplest for continuous or silent chanting, while the longer chant has a melodic rhythm suited to aloud japa. Follow your tradition or personal preference.

Can I use this counter for Ram naam lekhan?

You can use the printable 108 sheet on this page as a model for handwriting, and the counter to count your written repetitions with a tap after each one.

Is the Ram naam jap counter free?

Can I chant Ram naam at night?

Yes. Ram naam can be chanted at any time. Many people chant a few rounds before sleeping to end the day peacefully.
