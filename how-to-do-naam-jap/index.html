How To Do Naam Jap – A Complete Beginner Guide

Naam jap is the simplest sadhana: repeat the divine name with love. This guide shows you exactly how to begin, what to avoid and how to keep going.

What is naam jap?

Naam jap (also naam japa or naam jaap) means repeating the divine name – Radha, Krishna, Ram, Shiva, Hari, Durga, Hanuman or any name dear to you. Traditions say that the name and the named are not different, so repeating the name brings the presence of the divine to the mind.

Before you start: choose your name

If you have a guru, use the name given. If not, pick a name that you feel drawn to and that your family or community already honours: Radha, Ram, Hare Krishna, Om Namah Shivaya. Stay with the same name for at least forty days before changing.

Step-by-step method

  1. Fix a time. Early morning (Brahma Muhurta) is ideal; evening or bedtime are also good. Consistency matters more than the exact hour.
  2. Fix a place. A quiet corner, preferably clean, with a cushion or mat. A small lamp or picture helps.
  3. Sit well. Cross-legged or on a chair, spine tall, hands relaxed. Do not strain.
  4. Settle the breath. Two minutes of sama vritti pranayama.
  5. Set a sankalp. For example one mala (108). Use the 108 japa counter.
  6. Chant. Aloud (vaikhari), whisper (upamshu) or mental (manasika). Keep a gentle, even pace.
  7. Return gently. When your mind wanders, bring it back without judgement.
  8. Close. Sit silently for a minute, offer the japa to the divine and say thank you.

How long should I chant?

Begin with five to ten minutes. Increase gradually. The mantra calculator shows how time changes with the number of malas.

Common mistakes

  • Starting with a huge target and quitting in a week.
  • Chanting while checking the phone.
  • Changing mantra every few days.
  • Rushing the words.
  • Treating japa only as a way to get something, instead of a way to connect.

Build the habit

Tie japa to a daily trigger, track it, and forgive missed days. Over weeks, the practice becomes natural. Use the streak chart in the counter for motivation.

Frequently asked questions

How do I start naam jap as a beginner?

Choose a name, fix a time and place, sit calmly, set a small target like 108 and chant with attention, using a counter or mala.

Do I need a guru to do naam jap?

Naam jap of a simple divine name can be done by anyone. If you wish for formal initiation into a specific mantra, find a qualified guru.

Can I do naam jap lying down or walking?

Yes, naam jap can be done at any time, even while walking, though sitting practice is best for focus.

How many times should I do naam jap?

108 times daily is a common start. Increase slowly as you are comfortable.

What if my mind wanders?

This is normal. Gently bring attention back to the name. Returning is the practice.
