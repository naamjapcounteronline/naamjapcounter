What is the Om Namah Shivaya mantra?

Om Namah Shivaya, often searched as "om namah shivay" or "namasivaya", is the five-syllable (panchakshari) mantra of Lord Shiva. It is among the most widely chanted mantras in the world. The five syllables Na, Ma, Shi, Va, Ya, together with the sacred Om, form the six-syllable (shadakshari) version. The words "Namah Shivaya" appear in the Sri Rudram of the Yajur Veda, and the mantra is central to Shaiva traditions, especially in South India, where it is also known as Namasivaya.

Meaning of Om Namah Shivaya word by word

WordMeaning
OmThe primordial sound; the seed of all mantras
NamahI bow, salutations, not mine but yours
ShivayaTo Shiva, the auspicious one, the inner consciousness

Taken together the mantra means "I bow to Shiva, the auspicious one". Teachers often add that Shiva here is not only a deity in a temple but also the stillness and pure awareness within the one who chants. Traditions also link the five syllables with the five great elements (earth, water, fire, air and space); this is a symbolic interpretation found in several Shaiva commentaries.

Best time and days for Shiva japa

Traditionally, Monday is dedicated to Lord Shiva, and many devotees chant on Pradosh (twilight on the thirteenth lunar day), on Maha Shivaratri and throughout the month of Shravan. Early morning in Brahma Muhurta is a favourite hour for quiet japa. The mantra is also chanted at any other time by those who simply love it.

Benefits devotees associate with Om Namah Shivaya

Chanters most often describe a calmer mind, reduced restlessness and a sense of surrender and trust. In tradition the mantra is said to purify and protect. Scientific research on mantra meditation is still emerging, so these are best understood as commonly reported experiences rather than guaranteed outcomes. If you struggle with anxiety, sleep or health issues, please also consult a qualified professional.

The five syllables, their source and how to chant them

Source: "Namah Shivaya" appears in the Shri Rudram (Namakam) of the Yajurveda, and the five-syllable panchakshara mantra is discussed at length in the Shiva Purana. Om is added in front when chanting with the full six syllables.

Pronunciation: Om (long "oh-m"), Na-mah (the final "h" is a soft breath), Shi-vaa-ya, with the "va" long. Say it slowly: Om Nah-mah Shi-vaa-yah.

Frequently asked questions

How many times should I chant Om Namah Shivaya?

Many people chant 108 times daily. On Mondays, Pradosh or Shivaratri, 1008 or more is common. There is no compulsory number; consistency matters most.

What is the difference between Om Namah Shivaya and Namah Shivaya?

Namah Shivaya is the five-syllable form and Om Namah Shivaya adds the sacred syllable Om at the beginning. Both are widely chanted, and your tradition may prefer one form.

Can I chant Om Namah Shivaya at night?

Yes. The mantra can be chanted any time. Many chant a mala before sleep for a calm end to the day.
